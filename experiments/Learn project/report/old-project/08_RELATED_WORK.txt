[2] Dual-Distribution Discrepancy for Anomaly Detection in Chest X-Rays (DDAD)
    Cai et al. — MICCAI 2022; extended journal version in Medical Image Analysis
    arXiv 2206.03935
    [VERIFIED] Uses known-normal PLUS unlabeled images. Inter-discrepancy between
    two module ensembles + intra-discrepancy within the normal-only module as the
    anomaly score. Evaluated on three CXR datasets including RSNA.
    >> Same group as [1]. Strong, directly comparable, RSNA numbers available.

[3] Exploiting Structural Consistency of Chest Anatomy for Unsupervised Anomaly
    Detection in Radiography Images  (SimSID; earlier version SQUID)
    Xiang et al. — IEEE TPAMI 2024 (IF ~20) ; SQUID at CVPR 2023
    arXiv 2403.08689 | SQUID arXiv 2111.13495 | code: github.com/tiangexiang/SQUID
    [VERIFIED] Space-aware memory queue + deep feature in-painting. Exploits the
    fact that chest anatomy is spatially CONSISTENT across patients.
    SQUID: beats 13 SOTA UAD methods by >=5 AUC points on two CXR benchmarks.
    SimSID: +8.0 / +5.0 / +9.9 AUC on ZhangLab / COVIDx / CheXpert.
    >> WHY IT MATTERS TO YOU: this is the strongest counter-argument to your
       "lung prior is a shortcut" finding — they EXPLOIT spatial consistency
       deliberately and successfully. You must position against it: their spatial
       prior is learned per-region and used for in-painting; yours was an
       unintended geometric shortcut that inflated the pooled pixel metric.
