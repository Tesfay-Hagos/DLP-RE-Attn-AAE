DL PROJECT — OVERVIEW AND CURRENT STATE
Unsupervised anomaly detection on chest radiographs:
where do reconstruction methods actually get their accuracy from?

Dataset : RSNA Pneumonia Detection Challenge (chest X-ray), image-level
Task    : anomaly CLASSIFICATION (AnoCls) — one score per image, no labels in training
Code    : experiments/Learn project/DL/dl_project.py  (single self-contained notebook)

STATUS  : all planned experiments complete on RSNA. Four adversarial follow-ups
          (M0/M2/M4/M3) have been run and retracted four earlier claims.
          The one thing still missing is a SECOND DATASET.
          Read 11_CONCLUSIONS.txt for the current, corrected position.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE QUESTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every reconstruction-based anomaly detector makes two separable decisions:

    (a) the TRAINING OBJECTIVE   — what the model is optimised to reconstruct
    (b) the ANOMALY SCORE        — how the residual is turned into one number

In the literature these are almost always changed TOGETHER and reported under a single
name: AE-SSIM (Bergmann et al., 2019) trains and scores with SSIM; AE-PL (Shvetsova et
al., 2021) trains and scores with a VGG feature distance; AE-U (Mao et al., 2020) trains
with an uncertainty-weighted loss and scores with the same weighting. When AE-SSIM beats
a plain AE, the published number cannot say which decision earned it.

    THIS PROJECT SEPARATES (a) FROM (b) AND MEASURES EACH.

The gradient-score family (Zimmerer et al., 2019) does hold the objective fixed and vary
the score — but only along the gradient axis, never across reconstruction metrics. That
prior work shows the question is worth asking; nobody has asked it for the metrics.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHERE WE LANDED — the one mechanism
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An objective does not make the reconstruction better. It CONSTRAINS A SUBSPACE, and a
score can only read what was constrained. The relation is ASYMMETRIC:

    pixel-constrained model  (L2, SSIM)  -> readable by pixel AND feature scores
    feature-constrained model (perceptual) -> readable by feature scores ONLY

Evidence, and the single pattern it all reduces to (AUC):

    trained        l2     grad   perc   ssim   unc     below chance
    l2           68.3    73.9   75.9   77.4   82.0     none
    ssim         61.9    75.2   68.2   80.8   87.0     none
    perceptual   44.9    64.9   87.6   44.0   57.0     l2, ssim   <-- the only row
    aeu          65.4    71.7   73.6   75.1   86.9     none

Only the perceptually-trained row breaks, and only under pixel-space scores — because
perceptual loss does not constrain absolute pixel intensity, so the reconstruction is
free to drift there. This one mechanism explains the A1 grid, AE-U's advantage being in
its score rather than its reconstruction, AND the post-hoc head's behaviour across
backbones (it is a pixel-space score, so it lifts pixel-trained models and destroys the
perceptual one).

NOTE "the diagonal always wins" is FALSE: the L2 model is better read by SSIM
(77.4 > 68.3), and the SSIM model better by the uncertainty score (86.99 > 80.81).
Within a compatible space, scores transfer freely and can beat the native one.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HEADLINE RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R1  THE BASELINES REPRODUCE. 9 of 10 methods within one standard deviation of their
    published values, four within 0.2 AUC points. (DAE is the exception, n=1.)

R2  THE SCORE CARRIES MOST OF THE REPORTED GAIN — but the two factors interact, and the
    interaction is the largest term (+9.8 AE-SSIM, +35.1 AE-PL). A plain autoencoder
    RESCORED with SSIM reaches 77.4 against its own 68.35, with no retraining
    (+9.08, paired DeLong p=7.4e-37).

R3  AE-U's ADVANTAGE IS ITS SCORE, NOT ITS RECONSTRUCTION. Read with plain L2 it gives
    65.4 — below the plain autoencoder it beats by 18.5 points.

R4  UNCERTAINTY IS LARGELY RETROFITTABLE, WITH LIMITS. A variance head on a FROZEN
    autoencoder reaches 82.00 (+13.65, p=5e-75). It is not a static mask (69.10) and not
    zero-parameter whitening (69.59) — but it is dominated by a per-image SCALE rather
    than spatial structure (a flat map alone gives 79.87), it needs NONLINEARITY rather
    than capacity (808 nonlinear params 78.27 vs 8,192 linear 66.75), and it does not
    beat jointly-trained AE-U (4.86 short, p=4.3e-09).

R5  AN IMAGENET-FREE ROUTE TO AE-U's ACCURACY. Frozen AE-SSIM + post-hoc head = 86.99,
    against jointly-trained AE-U's 86.86, with no pre-trained weights.

R6  TWO COMPLEMENTARY SCORES BEAT THE BEST SINGLE IMAGE-RECONSTRUCTION METHOD.
    perceptual + uncertainty = 88.74 vs AE-PL's 87.58, surviving selection-aware
    validation (100% of 150 split-half selections). Reported with its negative control:
    both PRE-REGISTERED equal-weight ensembles LOST (84.88, 81.91).

WE DO NOT BEAT THE STATE OF THE ART. On this split FAE-SSIM reaches 91.1 (MedIAnomaly
Table 6), a feature-reconstruction method. Every claim here is scoped to
IMAGE-reconstruction methods, whose best published result on this split is 87.5.

5. WHAT IS NEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE GAP: every number above is RSNA. The findings are currently observations about one
dataset, not claims about a method family. A second dataset is the single highest-value
remaining item, and it is cheap.

MEASURED COST per AE-cost run, scaled from RSNA's 1.6 min on a T4 (cost tracks
train_size x epochs, NOT epochs alone):

    dataset        train  epochs   img-epochs   rel   min/run
    RSNA (done)    3,851     250      962,750  1.00      1.60
    VinDr-CXR      4,000     250    1,000,000  1.04      1.66
    Brain Tumor    1,000     600      600,000  0.62      1.00   <- CHEAPER than RSNA
    LAG            1,500     250      375,000  0.39      0.62   <- cheapest
    BraTS2021      4,211     250    1,052,750  1.09      1.75

Brain Tumor's 600 epochs are offset by having only 1,000 training images, so it costs
LESS per run than RSNA — an earlier estimate of "2.4x more expensive" was wrong.

WHY THE DATASET CHOICE IS SCIENTIFIC, NOT JUST BUDGETARY: §3's mechanism makes a
FALSIFIABLE PREDICTION. The post-hoc head is a pixel-space score that up-weights regions
where the frozen model reconstructs WELL. On Brain Tumor, lesions frequently sit where
the autoencoder already reconstructs badly, so the head should DOWN-weight the evidence
and FAIL. A correctly predicted failure would convert §3 from a hypothesis into a tested
theory — worth more than a third success on another chest X-ray dataset.
