SOURCE OF THE LIST
  Entries marked [BM] are the papers the benchmark study (Cai et al., 2025) itself
  cites for these methods and concepts — extracted from its reference list, not
  reconstructed from memory. Following its citations keeps our attribution consistent
  with the study our numbers are compared against.
  Entries marked [OURS] are components our implementation uses that the benchmark does
  not cite (U-Net, VGG-19, Adam, the uncertainty-loss lineage, the dataset papers).

VERIFICATION
  Every DOI below was resolved directly against api.crossref.org/works/{doi} and every
  arXiv id against export.arxiv.org, on 2026-08-11. For each, the returned title and
  first author were checked against the intended paper: 17/17 DOIs and 10/10 arXiv ids
  confirmed. Section 6 records the traps found along the way.

2. THE CONCEPTS AND LOSS FUNCTIONS THOSE METHODS ARE BUILT FROM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[7] SSIM — the similarity metric itself                                      [BM]
    Wang, Z., Bovik, A.C., Sheikh, H.R., Simoncelli, E.P.
    "Image Quality Assessment: From Error Visibility to Structural Similarity."
    IEEE Transactions on Image Processing 13(4), 600–612, 2004.
    DOI: 10.1109/TIP.2003.819861
    USED FOR: the 1-SSIM objective and score; local mean/variance/covariance windows.

[8] Perceptual loss — comparing deep features instead of pixels              [BM]
    Johnson, J., Alahi, A., Fei-Fei, L.
    "Perceptual Losses for Real-Time Style Transfer and Super-Resolution."
    ECCV 2016.
    DOI: 10.1007/978-3-319-46475-6_43
    USED FOR: the idea AE-PL adapts to anomaly detection.

[9] LPIPS — deep features as a perceptual similarity metric                 [OURS]
    Zhang, R., Isola, P., Efros, A.A., Shechtman, E., Wang, O.
    "The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Deep Features as a Perceptual Metric."
    CVPR 2018.
    DOI: 10.1109/CVPR.2018.00068
    USED FOR: the evidence that feature distances track perceptual similarity, which
    is the premise AE-PL relies on.

[10] VGG-19 — the feature extractor inside AE-PL                            [OURS]
     Simonyan, K., Zisserman, A.
     "Very Deep Convolutional Networks for Large-Scale Image Recognition."
     arXiv:1409.1556, 2014 (ICLR 2015).
     No DOI. The benchmark writes "VGG19-IN" without citing this paper; our report
     should cite it, since relu4_2 features are the mechanism of the method.

[11] Heteroscedastic mean-and-variance regression — the FORM of AE-U's loss  [OURS]
     Nix, D.A., Weigend, A.S.
     "Estimating the mean and variance of the target probability distribution."
     IEEE ICNN 1994.
     DOI: 10.1109/ICNN.1994.374138
     USED FOR: the exp(-log_var)*(x-x_hat)^2 + log_var objective.

[12] Aleatoric uncertainty in deep networks — the modern formulation         [OURS]
     Kendall, A., Gal, Y.
     "What Uncertainties Do We Need in Bayesian Deep Learning for Computer Vision?"
     arXiv:1703.04977, 2017 (NeurIPS 2017).
     No DOI. USED FOR: why per-pixel predicted variance is the right object to divide
     the residual by.

[13] U-Net — the backbone DAE uses                                          [OURS]
     Ronneberger, O., Fischer, P., Brox, T.
     "U-Net: Convolutional Networks for Biomedical Image Segmentation."
     MICCAI 2015.
     DOI: 10.1007/978-3-319-24574-4_28
     The benchmark describes DAE's "customised UNet" without citing this paper.

[14] Adam — the optimiser used for every run                                [OURS]
     Kingma, D.P., Ba, J.
     "Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization."
     arXiv:1412.6980, 2014 (ICLR 2015).  No DOI.

5. COMPARATIVE STUDIES — context, and our reference column
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[21] Cai, Y., Zhang, W., Chen, H., Cheng, K.-T.
     "MedIAnomaly: A comparative study of anomaly detection in medical images."
     Medical Image Analysis, 2025.
     DOI: 10.1016/j.media.2025.103500      arXiv: 2404.04518
     ROLE IN THIS PROJECT, precisely: supplies (i) the fixed RSNA split, (ii) the
     shared architecture and hyperparameters, (iii) the reference column our
     reproduction is checked against, and (iv) the citation list above. Method
     descriptions in our report come from [1]-[6], not from this study.

[22] Baur, C., Denner, S., Wiestler, B., Navab, N., Albarqouni, S.                [BM]
     "Autoencoders for Unsupervised Anomaly Segmentation in Brain MR Images:
      A Comparative Study."
     Medical Image Analysis 69, 101952, 2021.
     DOI: 10.1016/j.media.2020.101952
     RELEVANT BECAUSE: the earlier comparative study of the same family; found AE
     variants perform closely to one another, which our "flat cluster" echoes.

[23] Meissen, F., Wiestler, B., Kaissis, G., Rueckert, D.                    [OURS]
     "On the Pitfalls of Using the Residual Error as Anomaly Score."
     arXiv:2202.03826, 2022 (MIDL 2022).  No DOI.
     RELEVANT BECAUSE: the direct argument that the pixel residual is a poor anomaly
     score. Our A1 grid quantifies exactly this; the below-chance cell is an extreme case.

[24] Lagogiannis, I., Meissen, F., Kaissis, G., Rueckert, D.                 [OURS]
     "Unsupervised Pathology Detection: A Deep Dive Into the State of the Art."
     IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging, 2024.
     DOI: 10.1109/TMI.2023.3298093
     RELEVANT BECAUSE: independent evidence that rankings in this field are protocol-
     sensitive — the motivation for reproducing before extending.

6. VERIFICATION LOG — traps found, so they are not repeated
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
V1. TWO AUTHOR-LIST ERRORS IN THE EARLIER DRAFT, both found by comparing against the
    benchmark's own reference list and the DOI records:
      * Zimmerer et al. 2019 — correct order is Zimmerer, Isensee, Petersen, Kohl,
        Maier-Hein (the earlier draft had Kohl and Isensee transposed).
      * Kascenas et al. 2023 — the earlier draft invented "Papież, O'Neil" as
        co-authors. Correct list is in [6].

V2. TITLE SEARCHES THAT RESOLVE TO THE WRONG PAPER — always resolve by DOI/arXiv id:
      * "Abnormality Detection in Chest X-ray Images Using Uncertainty Prediction
        Autoencoders" returns a different 2021 paper by Saparkhojayev et al.
        (10.1109/icecco53203.2021.9663852). The correct work is [3].
      * "Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes" returns an unrelated 2024 item
        (10.61603/ceas.v2i1.33). Use arXiv:1312.6114.
      * "U-Net: Convolutional Networks for Biomedical Image Segmentation" also returns
        a 2017 invited-talk abstract by the same author
        (10.1007/978-3-662-54345-0_3). Use 10.1007/978-3-319-24574-4_28.
      * "Very Deep Convolutional Networks..." returns a super-resolution paper by Kim
        et al. Use arXiv:1409.1556.
      * "Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization" returns an R package.
        Use arXiv:1412.6980.

V3. VENUES WITH NO DOI — ICLR, NeurIPS, MIDL/PMLR and arXiv preprints. Cite the arXiv
    id; do not attach a DOI that was not issued. Affected here: [5], [10], [12], [14],
    [16], [18], [23], and the MIDL version of [6].

V4. CONFIRMED 2026-08-11: 17/17 DOIs and 10/10 arXiv ids resolve to the intended paper
    with matching first author. Re-run the check before submission if entries change.
