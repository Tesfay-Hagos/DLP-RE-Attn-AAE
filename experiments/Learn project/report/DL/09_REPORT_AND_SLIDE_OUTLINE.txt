--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART A — THE ONE-SENTENCE STORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    "Reconstruction-based anomaly detectors are always presented as a single method,
     but each is really two decisions — what to optimise and how to read the residual.
     We separate them, and find the second decision carries most of the accuracy."

Everything below is in service of that sentence. If a figure or paragraph does not
advance it, cut it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART B — WRITTEN REPORT OUTLINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. INTRODUCTION  (~1 page)
   - Clinical framing: abnormal findings are rare, diverse, expensive to annotate;
     normal studies are plentiful. Hence train on normal only.
   - The reconstruction premise, in one paragraph.
   - The gap: objective and score are always changed together and reported as one name.
   - Contributions, as three bullets:
       (i)   a faithful reproduction of 10 methods under one protocol,
       (ii)  a factorial separation of objective from score,
       (iii) two consequences of that separation — a free ensemble that beats the best
             single method, and a post-hoc uncertainty wrapper.

2. BACKGROUND AND RELATED WORK  (~1.5 pages)
   Structure it by MECHANISM, not chronology. Use 01_METHODS.txt.
   2.1 The reconstruction baseline and why pixel error is weak on radiographs [R2]
   2.2 Changing the metric: SSIM [M1, F1], perceptual [M2, F2, F3, F4]
   2.3 Changing the latent space: VAE [M5]
   2.4 Changing the score alone: gradient methods [M4]  <- credit this explicitly, it is
       the prior art closest to our contribution
   2.5 Modelling uncertainty: AE-U [M3], and its roots [F5, F6]
   2.6 Changing the input: denoising [M6], and the U-Net it uses [F7]
   2.7 Comparative studies [R1, R3, B1] and where this work differs from them

3. METHOD  (~2 pages)
   3.1 Dataset and split [D1, D2] — stress that the split is fixed and inherited
   3.2 Shared architecture and hyperparameters — stress that nothing was tuned
   3.3 The four objectives and five scores, with equations (01_METHODS.txt)
   3.4 The A1 factorial design, and the two cells that are structurally undefined
   3.5 A3: rank averaging, and why pre-registration was necessary
   3.6 A4: the frozen-model protocol and the capacity control

4. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP  (~0.5 page)
   Seeds, metrics, hardware, run bookkeeping (02_PROTOCOL.txt section 6).
   Include the config-fingerprint guard — it is a genuine methodological strength and
   distinguishes this from a notebook full of uncontrolled runs.

5. RESULTS  (~3 pages)
   5.1 Reproduction table + the three groupings (03)
   5.2 A1 grid, decomposition table, the four findings (04)
   5.3 A3: the failed pre-registered ensembles FIRST, then the surviving pair, then the
       selection-aware validation (05)
   5.4 A4: the capacity curve and the 26% that does not close (06)

6. DISCUSSION  (~1 page)
   - Why the score dominates: the objective shapes the residual rather than improving it
   - Why co-adaptation explains A4's irreducible gap
   - Practical implications: rescore before retraining; uncertainty is cheap to add
   - Threats to validity, in full (07 section 4). Do not bury these.

7. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK  (~0.5 page)
   Multi-dataset validation as the clear next step (07, O1).

PART C — SLIDE DECK (12-14 slides, ~15 min)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

S1  TITLE
    "Where does reconstruction-based anomaly detection actually get its accuracy?"
    RSNA chest X-rays / unsupervised / 10 methods reproduced + 3 new experiments

S2  THE PROBLEM
    One chest X-ray, normal vs pneumonia, side by side.
    "Train on normal only. Flag what reconstructs badly."

S3  THE CATCH  — the key setup slide
    A two-column diagram:
        TRAINING OBJECTIVE  |  ANOMALY SCORE
        what we optimise    |  how we read the residual
    "AE-SSIM changes BOTH. So does AE-PL. So does AE-U.
     Which one earns the +13?"

S4  BASELINES REPRODUCE
    The reproduction table, with the delta column highlighted.
    One line: "9 of 10 within one standard deviation, four within 0.2."
    Purpose of the slide: earn the right to be believed later.

S5  THE FLAT CLUSTER
    Bar chart, methods sorted. Colour the 67-69 cluster one colour, the 80+ group another.
    "Changing the bottleneck does nothing. Changing the metric does everything."

S6  EXPERIMENT A1 — THE GRID           <- the centrepiece slide
    The 4x5 heatmap. Diagonal boxed.
    Two numbers at the bottom: rows 14.2, columns 26.7.

S7  A1 — THREE THINGS THE GRID SAYS
    1. Rescoring a plain AE with SSIM: 68.3 -> 77.4, free
    2. Mismatch is harmful: perceptual-trained, pixel-scored = 44.9, below chance
    3. AE-U scored with L2 = 65.4, BELOW the plain AE

S8  THE DECOMPOSITION
    The four-row table (total / score-only / train-only / interaction).
    "The objective doesn't make the reconstruction better. It makes the residual READABLE."

S9  EXPERIMENT A4 — CAN WE GET AE-U's SCORE WITHOUT ITS TRAINING?
    Diagram: frozen AE (padlock icon) + small trainable variance head.
    Capacity curve: 66.9 at w=1, plateau ~82 at w=2-8, AE-U's 86.9 as a dashed line.
    "74% recovered for 0.62% extra parameters. 26% needs joint training."

S10 EXPERIMENT A3 — COMBINING SCORES
    Show the FAILURE first: the two pre-registered combinations, both below the line.
    Then the winner: perceptual+uncertainty at 88.74.
    "We pre-registered. Both pre-registered choices lost. That's why you can believe
     the one that won."

S11 DID IT SURVIVE SELECTION?
    Three bars: oracle 88.64 / held-out 88.83 / AE-PL 87.66.
    "Selection bias: -0.20. Same pair wins in 100% of 150 splits."

S12 WHAT THIS MEANS
    - Rescore before you retrain
    - Uncertainty scoring is mostly a wrapper, and it is cheap
    - Naive ensembling is not safe; the right pair is

S13 LIMITS  — do not skip this slide
    One dataset. Image-level only. n=3 seeds. DAE at n=1. Balanced test set.

S14 NEXT
    Multi-dataset validation (~3 h): does perceptual+uncertainty still win on VinCXR,
    BrainTumor, LAG?

PART E — WRITING RULES FOR THIS PROJECT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    * Attribute every method to the paper that introduced it (08_REFERENCES.txt).
      Use the comparative study for the reference column only.
    * Never state a difference below ~1.5 AUC points as a difference.
    * Always give n. DAE is n=1 and must be labelled every time it appears.
    * Report the failures: the pre-registered ensembles, the w=1 head, the DAE gap.
      They are what make the successes credible.
    * Say "on RSNA" in every general-sounding sentence until the multi-dataset run exists.
