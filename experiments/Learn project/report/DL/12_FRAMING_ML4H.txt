################################################################################
# 2. ABSTRACT  (196 words)
################################################################################
Unsupervised anomaly detection on medical images is reported as a contest between named
methods, but every reconstruction-based detector bundles two separable decisions: the
objective the model is trained to reconstruct, and the rule that turns the residual into a
score. The literature moves them together. The one exception -- substituting a structural
similarity residual into models trained under pixel objectives -- is recommended in the
current state-of-the-art benchmark on the grounds that it improved every model it was
applied to. We test that claim. Reproducing ten methods under one protocol and crossing
four training objectives with five anomaly scores on three datasets (RSNA, VinDr-CXR, LAG;
64x64, three seeds), we find the substitution improves AUROC by 9.08 points on RSNA and
harms it by 2.37 and 10.54 points on the other two; all three are Holm-significant under
paired DeLong tests and they disagree in sign. Two further design-level comparisons
reverse across datasets while four replicate three of three. A variance head fitted to a
frozen autoencoder shows what an uncertainty score computes: residual relative to expected
difficulty. A configuration selected on RSNA and applied unchanged elsewhere gains 1.33
AUROC over the strongest single method (95% CI [0.41, 2.25]; six of six held-out seed
measurements positive). We do not reach the benchmark's best reported accuracy; we report
what it is made of.

KEYWORDS: anomaly detection, medical imaging, evaluation, reproducibility, autoencoders

NOTE: the 89.46 three-member ensemble does NOT appear (it reverses on LAG, -1.26).

################################################################################
# 3. THE GAP  (use this wording)
################################################################################
In a reconstruction-based anomaly detector the reconstruction metric appears TWICE -- once
as the objective the model is trained under, and once as the distance that converts the
residual into a score -- and the literature moves the two together. Bergmann et al. state
the design explicitly ("by adapting the loss AND evaluation functions") and then attribute
the resulting 0.688 -> 0.966 AUC gain to "merely changing the loss function". MedIAnomaly
is the first to tabulate loss and score as separate columns, yet instantiates them with the
same distance in every reconstruction method it evaluates. The off-diagonal has been
entered exactly once, unsystematically: Lagogiannis et al. substitute an SSIM residual into
models trained under pixel objectives and report that it helped every model it was applied
to. No work in any domain -- medical, industrial, or video -- evaluates the two axes as a
crossed design, so no published gain in this family is attributable to the objective, to
the score, or to their interaction.

POSITIONING SENTENCE FOR RELATED WORK (replaces 10 §3's, which is factually wrong):
    "Baur et al. and Cai et al. hold the residual distance fixed while varying
     architecture, bottleneck, or extraction paradigm; Cai et al. are the first to tabulate
     the training loss and the anomaly score as separate columns, but in every
     reconstruction method they evaluate the two are instantiated with the same distance
     function. The single exception in the literature is Lagogiannis et al., who substitute
     an SSIM residual into four models trained under pixel objectives and report that this
     'increased the performance of all selected models it was applied to' -- one
     off-diagonal cell per model, reported as a preprocessing decision rather than as a
     result. We evaluate the full cross, and find that this substitution's sign is
     dataset-dependent."

NEVER WRITE (each is false and a reviewer can produce the counterexample):
    x "first to vary the anomaly score"                    -> Zimmerer et al. 2019
    x "no prior work crosses the reconstruction metric"    -> Lagogiannis §V-D

SAFE:

    + "first to attribute published gains to objective, score, and interaction"

################################################################################
# 4. RESEARCH QUESTION
################################################################################
PRIMARY
    When a reconstruction-based medical anomaly detector reports a gain, which of its two
    coupled design decisions -- the training objective or the anomaly score -- earned that
    gain, and does the answer hold when the same decision is applied to a different dataset?

RQ1  Crossing objectives with scores factorially on one backbone and one protocol, how much
     of each published method's advantage is attributable to the score, to the objective,
     and to their interaction?
RQ2  Which attributions replicate across three datasets and which reverse -- and are the
     reversals large and significant enough to be findings rather than noise?
RQ3  What does an uncertainty-weighted residual score actually compute, and does that
     mechanism predict where it will fail?
RQ4  Does any configuration selected on one dataset transfer, unchanged, to datasets it was
     not selected on?

# 5. HYPOTHESES  (each falsifiable, each mapped to evidence, each with a verdict)
################################################################################
H1  THE ANOMALY SCORE IS A SEPARABLE, TRANSFERABLE LEVER.
    Falsifiable form: rescoring fixed trained weights with a different reconstruction
    metric changes AUROC in a CONSISTENT DIRECTION across datasets.
    Evidence: +9.08 / -2.37 / -10.54, all Holm-significant.
    VERDICT: REFUTED — AND THIS REFUTATION IS THE PAPER. The magnitude claim survives
    (a free rescore moves AUROC by up to 10.5 points); the direction claim does not.
    Both directions significant on the same test => not noise, not a power failure.

H2  OBJECTIVE AND SCORE INTERACT; MISMATCHING THEM IS ACTIVELY HARMFUL.
    Falsifiable form: a model trained under one metric and scored with plain L2 will not
    fall below a plain autoencoder.
    Evidence: every such cell falls below (61.9 SSIM-trained, 65.4 uncertainty-trained,
    44.9 perceptual-trained); interactions +9.8 and +35.1 exceed both main effects.
    VERDICT: SUPPORTED. 44.9 is BELOW CHANCE.

H3  UNCERTAINTY TRAINING PRODUCES A BETTER RECONSTRUCTOR.
    Evidence: AE-U scored with L2 = 65.4 vs plain AE 68.35.
    VERDICT: REFUTED. Its entire advantage lives in the scoring rule.

H4  A POST-HOC VARIANCE HEAD BEATS THE FROZEN BACKBONE IT IS FITTED ONTO.
    Evidence: vs plain AE +13.65 / +12.38 / +4.31; vs AE-SSIM +1.19 / +13.95 / +15.92.
    VERDICT: SUPPORTED 3/3. THE MOST ROBUST FINDING IN THE PAPER.

H5  THE JOINT-VS-POST-HOC GAP IS CAUSED BY RECONSTRUCTION/VARIANCE CO-ADAPTATION.
    Falsifiable form: jointly trained AE-U exceeds frozen-backbone-plus-head on EVERY
    dataset.
    Evidence: +4.86 / +5.72 / -0.78 (LAG, p_holm=0.055).
    VERDICT: REFUTED AS STATED. Co-adaptation predicts a gap everywhere; LAG shows none.
    This was OUR OWN proposed mechanism. Report the falsification — it is worth more than
    the original claim.

H6  THE UNCERTAINTY SCORE MEASURES RECONSTRUCTION ERROR MAGNITUDE.
    Evidence: corr(log_var, residual) = +0.506 (far from 1); log_var(abnormal) -
    log_var(normal) = -0.0997, OPPOSITE in sign to the residual difference.
    VERDICT: REFUTED, AND THE REFUTATION IS THE MECHANISM — residual relative to EXPECTED
    difficulty. Normal-but-hard: large residual, large log_var, ratio moderate.
    Abnormal: large residual, SMALL log_var, ratio large.

H7  A CONFIGURATION SELECTED ON ONE DATASET TRANSFERS UNCHANGED TO UNSEEN DATASETS.
    Evidence: RSNA +1.16 (in-sample), VinCXR +1.57, LAG +1.08 (both held out);
    pooled held-out +1.33, 95% CI [+0.41,+2.25], t=3.70 df=5 p=0.0140; 6/6 positive.
    VERDICT: SUPPORTED — with the unit-of-analysis caveat IN THE SAME BREATH: dataset as
    unit gives n=2, which supports 2/2 agreement and NO p-value.

H8  THE THREE-MEMBER EXTENSION ALSO TRANSFERS.
    Evidence: +0.72 (RSNA, selected here) / +0.82 (VinCXR) / -1.26 (LAG, matched seeds).
    VERDICT: REFUTED ON HELD-OUT DATA. Keep out of every headline. Its RSNA number is
    exactly the selected-on-test artefact this paper argues against — say so.

-- PRIMARY --------------------------------------------------------------------

C1  [EMPIRICAL FINDING + CORRECTION TO PUBLISHED WORK]
    A benchmark-recommended, no-retraining score substitution reverses sign across
    datasets. Lagogiannis et al. report that replacing a pixel residual with an SSIM
    residual "increased the performance of all selected models it was applied to"; under
    paired DeLong with Holm correction we measure +9.08 (RSNA), -2.37 (VinCXR), -10.54
    (LAG), all significant.
    FALSIFY BY: showing the negative direction is an artefact of these two splits, or that
    it vanishes with >=10 seeds.

C2  [EMPIRICAL FINDING + NEGATIVE RESULT]
    A design-decision replication table: of seven design-level comparisons applied
    unchanged to three datasets, FOUR replicate 3/3 and THREE reverse. Replications and
    reversals appear in ONE table with equal prominence.
    FALSIFY BY: showing a replicating row reverses on a further dataset (which would
    strengthen the thesis while falsifying that row), or that a reversal is a loader bug.

C3  [METHODOLOGICAL / INSTRUMENT]
    The objective x score factorial as an attribution instrument. Crossing four objectives
    with five scores on one backbone and one protocol makes the two decisions individually
    testable and shows they are NOT additive — the interaction exceeds both main effects
    (+9.8 AE-SSIM, +35.1 AE-PL) — so no method that changes both can attribute its gain to
    either. Credit Lagogiannis for entering the off-diagonal; we claim the full cross.
    FALSIFY BY: showing the decomposition is unstable under a different backbone.

C4  [NEGATIVE RESULT]
    Mismatching objective and score is actively harmful, including below chance. Every
    metric-trained model scored with plain L2 falls below a plain autoencoder; a
    perceptually trained model scored with L2 reaches 44.9 — below chance, consistently
    across seeds — because the perceptual objective constrains deep features, not absolute
    intensity or contrast.
    FALSIFY BY: showing it disappears under per-image intensity normalisation before
    scoring (which would locate it in preprocessing, not the objective).

-- SUPPORTING -----------------------------------------------------------------

C5  [MECHANISM]
    What an uncertainty score computes: residual relative to expected difficulty.
    corr(log_var, residual) = +0.506, yet abnormal images get a SMALLER log_var (-0.0997)
    and are discounted LESS than their residual alone would predict.
    FALSIFY BY: showing the class contrast vanishes on a further dataset while the head
    still helps — decoupling the mechanism from the benefit.

C6  [EMPIRICAL FINDING]
    A genuinely held-out transfer result: head width and ensemble membership selected on
    RSNA, applied unchanged to VinCXR and LAG, beat the strongest single method by a pooled
    +1.33 AUROC. Reported ALONGSIDE its own failed extension (-1.26 on held-out LAG).
    FALSIFY BY: a negative gain on a further dataset, or non-survival at higher seed count.

C7  [ARTIFACT]
    Ten methods reproduced under one protocol on three datasets with matched parameter
    counts; a rescoring harness computing any (objective, score) cell from stored weights
    at ZERO training cost; the frozen-backbone variance-head probe; and per-image score
    vectors for every cell, so a third party can add an objective or a score WITHOUT A GPU.
    FALSIFY BY: re-running the released code and landing outside the reproduction band.

# 7. THE "WE DO NOT BEAT SOTA" PARAGRAPH  (use as written; never apologise)
################################################################################
The best result on this benchmark's RSNA split is FAE-SSIM at 91.1 AUROC, a
feature-reconstruction method built on ImageNet weights; the best result we measure is an
existing method. That is the correct outcome for this study, because a number and an
explanation are different products and this paper produces the second. FAE-SSIM changes the
representation and the score in one step, which is precisely the design our analysis shows
to be unattributable: its 91.1 cannot be assigned to its representation or to its scoring
rule by any evidence currently published, including its own. Our contribution is the
machinery that would make such an assignment possible, and the demonstration -- on three
datasets, with all three directions statistically significant -- that assignments made on
one dataset do not survive to the next. A practitioner who takes 91.1 from a table and
deploys it on their own data is making exactly the bet that our rescore row shows can cost
10.5 AUROC points. Every comparison here is internal to image-reconstruction detectors,
whose best published result on this split is 87.5; extending the factorial to
feature-reconstruction methods is the natural next step.

RULE: never write "although we do not beat SOTA". State the scope positively, once, early.

# 8. THE FIVE REJECTION SENTENCES, AND THE ANSWERS
################################################################################
R1 "This is an ablation of MedIAnomaly. Where is the novelty?"
   -> MedIAnomaly's unit of analysis is the METHOD; ours is the DESIGN DECISION inside it,
      and the two disagree: method rankings are stable across our three datasets while
      three of seven design-level comparisons reverse. And we correct a specific universal
      claim in the current SOTA benchmark (Lagogiannis §V-D).

R2 "You do not beat the state of the art."
   -> Correct, stated in the abstract. The product is the attribution of published numbers,
      and the benchmark's best number is itself unattributable by our argument.

R3 "Your reversals are noise — three seeds, and DeLong carries no seed variance."
   -> The reversals are 9-10.5 AUROC points against seed sds under one point, the sign is
      consistent across all three seeds within each dataset, and we report the SEED-LEVEL
      test alongside the paired one rather than resting on DeLong.

R4 "Three datasets, two of them chest X-ray, proves nothing about transfer."
   -> We claim no general law: we exhibit a specific, quantified, twice-significant FAILURE
      of transfer, which is sufficient to refute the implicit universality of
      single-dataset attribution. Dataset-as-unit is marked n=2 with no p-value.

R5 "64x64 and one small backbone says nothing about deployable systems."
   -> Resolution, architecture and hyperparameters are inherited unchanged from the
      benchmark precisely so the reproduction is valid and the factorial confounder-free.
      A study that retuned the backbone could not make this argument at all.
