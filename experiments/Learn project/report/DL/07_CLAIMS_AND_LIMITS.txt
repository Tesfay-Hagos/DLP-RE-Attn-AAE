================================================================================
CLAIMS AND LIMITS
What the evidence supports, what it does not, and what is still open.
Read this before writing a single sentence of the report.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. CLAIMS THAT ARE SUPPORTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C1. "We reproduce ten published anomaly detection methods on RSNA under one protocol;
     nine of ten fall within one standard deviation of their published values."
    EVIDENCE: 03_RESULTS_BASELINES.txt. Four within 0.2 points. Parameter counts match.

C2. "The anomaly score, not the training objective, carries most of the reported gain."
    EVIDENCE: A1 column spread 26.7 vs row spread 14.2; AE-Grad's +5.6 at 100% score;
    AE-SSIM's 73% score share.
    QUALIFY IT: this is an average over the grid, and it is NOT a claim that objectives
    are irrelevant — see C3.

C3. "Objective and score must match; mismatching them is actively harmful."
    EVIDENCE: every 'trained on a metric, scored with L2' cell falls below the plain AE;
    interaction terms (+9.8 for AE-SSIM, +35.1 for AE-PL) exceed both main effects.

C4. "A plain autoencoder rescored with SSIM captures 73% of AE-SSIM's advantage with no
     retraining." (68.3 -> 77.4)
    EVIDENCE: A1, 3 seeds.

C5. "Uncertainty training does not produce a better reconstructor."
    EVIDENCE: AE-U scored with plain L2 = 65.4, below the plain AE's 68.35, 3 seeds.

C6. "74% of AE-U's advantage is recoverable post hoc from a frozen autoencoder for 0.62%
     additional parameters; the remaining 26% does not close with more capacity."
    EVIDENCE: A4, plateau of 0.24 across a 12x capacity range.

C7. "A rank-average of the perceptual and uncertainty scores reaches 88.74, beating the
     best single method by 1.16, and survives selection-aware validation."
    EVIDENCE: A3, 150 split-half selections, wins 100%, selection bias -0.20.

C8. "Naive equal-weight score ensembling fails."
    EVIDENCE: both pre-registered combinations lost; subsets containing the weak L2
    member average 80.08 vs 86.44 without it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. CLAIMS THAT ARE *NOT* SUPPORTED — do not write these
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
X1. "This generalises to medical anomaly detection." ONE DATASET. Every number here is
    RSNA. The correct scope is "on chest radiographs in this benchmark's RSNA split".

X2. "Ensembling improves anomaly detection." It improved for one specific pair, and
    demonstrably hurt for most subsets. State the pair, not the principle.

X3. "AE-U is unnecessary / can be replaced by the post-hoc head." It cannot: 82.05 vs
    86.86, and the gap is stable across capacity.

X4. "Our DAE result contradicts the published one." n=1. A single seed cannot separate
    an implementation gap from seed variation.

X5. "AE-l1 / VAE are worse (or better) than AE." All within seed noise. Report as
    indistinguishable.

X6. "Larger post-hoc heads degrade performance." The w=16 drop is an optimisation
    failure (initial losses up to 54,824, +-4.77 spread), not a capacity effect.

X7. "The score is all that matters." The interaction terms are the largest components in
    the decomposition. The honest statement is that the objective's role is indirect.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. OPEN ITEMS, RANKED BY VALUE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
O1. MULTI-DATASET VALIDATION.  [highest value, ~3 h]
    Every finding is single-dataset. VinCXR, BrainTumor and LAG use the identical
    file layout, so the existing loader handles them with a changed name. At 1.6 min per
    run each dataset costs ~35 min. If perceptual+uncertainty wins there too, C7 becomes
    a general claim instead of an RSNA observation. If it does not, that is equally worth
    knowing and must be reported.

O2. VISUAL CHECK OF THE BELOW-CHANCE CELL.  [30 min]
    AE-PL scored with L2 gives 44.9 — below chance, consistently across 3 seeds. The
    proposed explanation is that perceptual training leaves a global intensity offset
    that dominates any pixel metric. Plot input / reconstruction / error map for an
    AE-PL model beside an AE one. If the reconstruction looks like a plausible
    radiograph at the wrong brightness, the explanation is confirmed and the finding
    becomes more interesting. Until then, report the number and label the explanation a
    hypothesis.

O3. TWO MORE DAE SEEDS.  [~2.7 h]
    Closes the only reproduction miss, or confirms it is real.

O4. MECHANISM OF THE LEARNED VARIANCE.  [~1 h]
    What does log_var actually key on — anatomy, edges, high-frequency texture? A figure
    correlating the predicted variance map with image structure would turn C5/C6 from an
    observation into an explanation. This is the piece most likely to lift the work from
    "measurement" to "understanding".

O5. WEIGHTED ENSEMBLING.  [~1 h]
    C8 says equal weights fail because of a weak member. The obvious follow-up is
    performance-weighted or rank-correlation-pruned combination. Cheap: no training.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THREATS TO VALIDITY — state these in the report
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
T1. SINGLE DATASET. See X1/O1.

T2. NO SEPARATE VALIDATION SET. The benchmark protocol trains for a fixed 250 epochs and
    evaluates on the test set; there is no held-out set for model selection, because the
    setting provides no labelled normal/abnormal data to select on. We inherit this. It
    is why A3 needed an explicit split-half protocol rather than a validation set.

T3. BALANCED TEST SET. 50% prevalence is a benchmark construction, not clinical reality.
    AP in particular would be far lower at realistic prevalence.

T4. IMAGE-LEVEL ONLY. Nothing here says where in the image the abnormality is. The
    scores are single numbers per image; no localisation claim is made or supported.

T5. n=3 SEEDS. Adequate for the large effects reported (+9 to +19 points), inadequate for
    sub-1.5-point differences. Every comparison in this project either clears that bar or
    is explicitly reported as indistinguishable.

T6. THE ARCHITECTURE AND HYPERPARAMETERS ARE INHERITED, NOT TUNED. This is deliberate
    (it is what makes the reproduction valid) but it means no claim is made about
    optimal settings, and the ranking could change under tuning.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. HONESTY NOTES TO CARRY INTO THE WRITE-UP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    * Report the FAILED pre-registered ensembles as prominently as the successful pair.
      The credibility of C7 rests on having pre-registered at all.
    * Report the w=1 post-hoc failure. It is what makes the capacity control meaningful.
    * Report the DAE gap and its n=1 status rather than omitting the row.
    * State that the gradient-score family already decouples objective from score. The
      contribution is crossing the reconstruction-metric axis, not the idea that the two
      are separable — that idea has prior art and should be credited.
    * The best cell in the A1 grid is AE-PL, an existing method. A1 and A4 EXPLAIN the
      state of the art; only A3 exceeds it, and only by 1.16 points on one dataset.
