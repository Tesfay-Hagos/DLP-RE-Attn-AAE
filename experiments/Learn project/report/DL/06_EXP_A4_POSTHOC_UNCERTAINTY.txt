================================================================================
EXPERIMENT A4 (NOVEL)
Is AE-U's advantage a training paradigm, or a post-hoc scoring wrapper?
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. MOTIVATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AE-U (Mao et al., 2020) is the most interesting method in the study: 86.86 AUC, within
0.7 of the ImageNet-dependent best, for 256 extra parameters and no pre-training.

A1 produced a sharp clue about WHY. AE-U scored with plain L2 gives 65.4 — BELOW the
plain autoencoder's 68.35. Uncertainty training does not build a better reconstructor;
it builds a slightly worse one. So the entire +18.5 must live in the scoring rule:
dividing the residual by the model's own predicted per-pixel variance.

But A1 could not close the argument. The complementary cell — a plain autoencoder scored
with the uncertainty rule — is undefined, because that score needs a log-variance output
only the AE-U backbone has.

    A4 obtains that cell by another route: build the missing log-variance head onto an
    ALREADY-TRAINED, FROZEN plain autoencoder.

If uncertainty is genuinely a scoring wrapper, it should be recoverable this way, and
joint training was never necessary. If it is genuinely a training paradigm, the frozen
reconstruction will not support it and the attempt will fail. Both outcomes are results.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. METHOD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    1. Take the plain autoencoder already trained in Step 1 for that seed.
    2. Freeze it completely: .eval() mode, requires_grad = False on every parameter.
       The reconstruction x_hat is fixed and never changes again.
    3. Attach a variance head that reads the autoencoder's last decoder feature map and
       emits a per-pixel log-variance at full resolution.
    4. Train ONLY that head, with AE-U's own objective, on the frozen reconstruction:

           L = mean_i [ exp(-log_var_i) * (x_i - x_hat_i)^2 + log_var_i ]

       The residual term is a constant with respect to the head's parameters; the head
       can only learn where the FIXED errors are expected to be large.
    5. Score exactly as AE-U does:

           s(x) = mean_i [ exp(-log_var_i) * (x_i - x_hat_i)^2 ]

    250 epochs, Adam lr 1e-3, 3 seeds — identical to every other run.

CRITICAL IMPLEMENTATION DETAIL
    The autoencoder is re-asserted into .eval() at the start of EVERY epoch. In train
    mode its BatchNorm layers would use batch statistics and mutate their running
    statistics, so the "frozen" model would silently drift while being measured, and the
    experiment would be measuring nothing.

THE CAPACITY CONTROL — without this the experiment is uninterpretable
    The head at its smallest is 256 parameters: exactly AE-U's parameter increment
    (2,351,440 - 2,351,184), so the comparison is fair on capacity.

    But the head reads features that were optimised for reconstruction alone and never
    for variance. A failure at 256 parameters would therefore have TWO explanations:
    uncertainty genuinely needs joint training, OR 256 parameters cannot extract it from
    features that do not encode it conveniently. These must be separated, so the head
    width is swept over five settings.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    head width   params     % of AE   AUC (%)          recovers
    ---------------------------------------------------------------
    plain AE     --         --        68.35 +- 0.65      0%   (the model being wrapped)
    w = 1        256        0.01%     66.94 +- 1.15     -8%   FAILS
    w = 2        14,528     0.62%     82.00 +- 0.83     74%
    w = 4        47,488     2.02%     82.05 +- 0.55     74%
    w = 8        168,704    7.18%     81.81 +- 0.71     73%
    w = 16       632,320    26.9%     78.22 +- 4.77     53%   (unstable, see 4.3)
    ---------------------------------------------------------------
    AE-U joint   256        0.01%     86.86 +- 0.44    100%

    "recovers" = (method - 68.35) / (86.86 - 68.35), the fraction of AE-U's advantage.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 UNCERTAINTY SCORING IS LARGELY A POST-HOC WRAPPER.
    A 14,528-parameter head — 0.62% of the autoencoder it sits on — fitted to a network
    whose weights are never touched, recovers 74% of AE-U's advantage: 82.00 against the
    plain autoencoder's 68.35, an increase of +13.65 for no reconstruction retraining.

    Practical consequence: an existing trained autoencoder can be upgraded substantially
    without retraining it. Note also that 82.00 BEATS fully-retrained AE-SSIM (80.81),
    and needs no ImageNet weights.

4.2 THE CAPACITY THRESHOLD IS SHARP, AND THEN IT PLATEAUS.
    Between w=1 and w=2 the result jumps +15.06 points. After that it is flat: 82.00,
    82.05, 81.81 across a 12x range of capacity, a spread of 0.24.

    The plateau is the evidence that matters. Three widths spanning an order of
    magnitude landing within a quarter of a point means the ceiling is NOT capacity
    starvation. Post-hoc extraction saturates at ~82.

4.3 4.81 POINTS (26%) ARE IRREDUCIBLE, AND THAT IS WHAT JOINT TRAINING BUYS.
    AE-U reaches 86.86; the best post-hoc head reaches 82.05. The gap does not close
    with more capacity. The natural reading: in joint training the reconstruction and
    the variance CO-ADAPT — the encoder is free to arrange its features so the variance
    is easy to predict, and the decoder is free to leave residuals the variance can
    explain. A frozen reconstruction forecloses that, and no amount of head capacity
    recovers it.

    So the honest three-part statement is:
        * uncertainty training does NOT improve the reconstruction (A1: 65.4 < 68.35)
        * 74% of its benefit is a scoring wrapper obtainable post hoc for 0.62% params
        * 26% requires the joint optimisation and cannot be retrofitted

4.4 THE w=16 RESULT IS AN OPTIMISATION FAILURE, NOT A CAPACITY RESULT — DO NOT REPORT
    IT AS EVIDENCE ABOUT CAPACITY.
    Initial training losses were 619, 3,381 and 54,824 for the three seeds; convergence
    took over 200 epochs; seed 44 finished at -4.01 where the others reached -4.06/-4.10,
    and scored 71.5 against their ~81.6. The +-4.77 spread is the giveaway.
    Cause: exp(-log_var) explodes when a large, poorly-conditioned head initialises
    log_var badly. This is a known fragility of the heteroscedastic objective, not a
    statement about how much capacity the task needs.
    Report widths 1-8; mention w=16 as a stability footnote.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT TO CLAIM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUPPORTED:
    "Most of AE-U's advantage over a plain autoencoder is a post-hoc scoring wrapper:
     a variance head fitted to a frozen autoencoder, costing 0.62% additional parameters
     and no reconstruction retraining, recovers 74% of it. The residual 26% does not
     close with additional head capacity and is attributable to the co-adaptation of
     reconstruction and variance during joint training."

DO NOT CLAIM:
    * that post-hoc equals AE-U — it does not, and the 4.81-point gap is stable
    * that 256 parameters are insufficient in principle — they are sufficient when
      trained jointly (that is precisely AE-U); they are insufficient only when reading
      frozen features
    * anything from w=16

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHY THIS IS THE MOST PUBLISHABLE PIECE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    A1 and A3 are analysis: they explain where existing methods' accuracy comes from.
    A4 converts that analysis into a METHOD — a recipe someone can apply to an
    autoencoder they already have — and it is the piece with a mechanism attached
    (co-adaptation) rather than only an observation.

    It also carries its own negative control (w=1 fails), its own capacity control
    (the plateau), and a documented stability caveat (w=16). That structure is what
    makes a small result defensible.
