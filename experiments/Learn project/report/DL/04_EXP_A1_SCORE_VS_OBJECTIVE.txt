================================================================================
EXPERIMENT A1 (NOVEL)
Separating the training objective from the anomaly score
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. MOTIVATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every metric-based method in Step 1 changed two things at once:

    AE-SSIM   trains on 1-SSIM        AND scores with 1-SSIM
    AE-PL     trains on perceptual    AND scores with perceptual
    AE-U      trains with uncertainty AND scores with uncertainty

So "AE-SSIM 80.9 vs AE 67.5" is a single number covering two distinct mechanisms, and
the published table cannot say which one earned the +13.4.

BE ACCURATE ABOUT THE PRIOR WORK. The gradient-score family (Zimmerer et al., 2019)
DOES decouple the two: AE-Grad has the same backbone, the same weights and the same
training as a plain AE, and differs only in the score — reaching 73.86 against 68.35.
So the idea that the score is a lever is established. What has never been done is
crossing the RECONSTRUCTION-METRIC axis: nobody has trained with SSIM and scored with
perceptual, or trained with L2 and scored with SSIM.

    A1 does exactly that.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Train an autoencoder with each of 4 objectives; score every trained model with each of
5 scoring functions. The diagonal reproduces the published methods; everything else
is new.

    training objectives : L2, SSIM, perceptual, uncertainty (AE-U)
    scoring functions   : L2, SSIM, perceptual, input-gradient, uncertainty

COST: 12 trainings (4 objectives x 3 seeds). The other 39 cells are RESCORES — the
stored weights are reloaded and the test set is scored again. No extra training at all.

TWO STRUCTURAL CONSTRAINTS, both honest limitations rather than oversights:
  * The uncertainty SCORE requires a predicted log-variance, which only the AE-U
    backbone has. That column exists for the AE-U row alone; the other three cells are
    undefined, not missing. The grid is therefore not fully crossed.
  * Consequently the "plain AE + uncertainty score" cell — the one that would isolate
    AE-U's score cleanly — cannot be computed here at all. Experiment A4 was built
    specifically to obtain it by another route.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Image AUC (%), mean over 3 seeds. Rows = what it was TRAINED with, columns = what it
was SCORED with. Diagonal cells (published methods) marked *.

                    |   L2     grad    percep   SSIM     uncert
    ----------------+--------------------------------------------
    trained L2      |  68.3*   73.9     75.9    77.4       --
    trained SSIM    |  61.9    75.2     68.2    80.8*      --
    trained percep  |  44.9    64.9     87.6*   44.0       --
    trained uncert  |  65.4    71.7     73.6    75.1      86.9*

    spread across TRAINING objectives (row means) : 14.2 AUC points
    spread across SCORING functions   (col means) : 26.7 AUC points

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. DECOMPOSITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For each published method, split its gain over the plain AE (68.3) into:
    score-only  = plain L2 model, rescored with that method's score
    train-only  = that method's model, rescored with plain L2
    interaction = total - score-only - train-only

  method     total   score-only   train-only   interaction   score share
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  AE-Grad    +5.6      +5.6          0.0          0.0          100%
  AE-SSIM   +12.5      +9.1         -6.4         +9.8           73%
  AE-U      +18.6       n/a          -2.9         n/a          >100%
  AE-PL     +19.3      +7.6         -23.4        +35.1           39%

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

F1. THE SCORE MATTERS ROUGHLY TWICE AS MUCH AS THE OBJECTIVE.
    Column spread 26.7 vs row spread 14.2. Averaged over models, changing how you read
    the residual moves accuracy about twice as far as changing what you optimised.

F2. A FREE +9.1: RESCORING A PLAIN AUTOENCODER.
    The plain L2 autoencoder, simply rescored with SSIM, reaches 77.4 against its own
    68.3. That is 73% of AE-SSIM's entire published advantage, obtained with NO
    retraining — the same weights, a different function applied at test time.
    Note also that for the L2-trained model, SSIM scoring beats L2 scoring: the best
    score for a plain autoencoder is not the loss it was trained with.

F3. MISMATCHING THE PAIR IS ACTIVELY HARMFUL, NOT MERELY SUBOPTIMAL.
    Every "trained on a metric, scored with L2" cell falls BELOW the plain AE:
        SSIM-trained scored L2        61.9   (-6.4)
        uncertainty-trained scored L2 65.4   (-2.9)
        perceptual-trained scored L2  44.9  (-23.4)  <- BELOW CHANCE
    The perceptually-trained model's pixel-space residual is ANTI-correlated with
    abnormality. Perceptual loss does not constrain absolute pixel intensity, so the
    reconstruction is free to carry a global intensity/contrast offset that dominates
    any pixel metric. (Worth confirming visually before publishing this specific number
    — see 07_CLAIMS_AND_LIMITS.txt.)

F4. THE INTERACTION IS LARGER THAN EITHER MAIN EFFECT.
    AE-PL's interaction term is +35.1, AE-SSIM's +9.8. The two decisions are not
    additive and the diagonal is the maximum for all three metric-trained models. The
    right interpretation of a training objective in this setting is therefore NOT "it
    makes the reconstruction better" — the train-only columns show it makes it WORSE by
    every pixel measure. It shapes WHAT KIND OF RESIDUAL is left behind, and only the
    matching metric can read that residual.

F5. AE-U's ADVANTAGE IS NOT A BETTER RECONSTRUCTION.
    AE-U scored with plain L2 gives 65.4 — below the plain autoencoder's 68.3. Whatever
    uncertainty training does, it does not produce a model that reconstructs normal
    anatomy better. Its entire +18.6 must therefore live in the scoring rule. This is
    the finding experiment A4 then tests constructively.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. ONE SENTENCE FOR THE REPORT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    "In reconstruction-based anomaly detection on chest radiographs, most of the
     accuracy attributed to a training objective is in fact delivered by the scoring
     function paired with it — but only when the two match, because the objective's
     real role is to shape the residual so that its own metric can read it."
