################################################################################
# 2. WHAT IS NOW ESTABLISHED
################################################################################

E1. THE VARIANCE HEAD IS NOT A STATIC ANATOMICAL MASK.            [hypothesis killed]
    The most dangerous alternative explanation was that, with a 16-d latent on
    globally-registered radiographs and a frozen residual as its only target, the head
    would collapse to the pixelwise mean training error — a fixed lung mask. Replacing
    log_var with exactly that map gives 69.10, against the full head's 82.00. The
    per-image component is worth +12.89 and the static component almost nothing.

E2. IT IS NOT REPRODUCIBLE WITH ZERO PARAMETERS.                  [hypothesis killed]
    Dividing the residual by the training-set mean squared residual — no head, no
    training — gives 69.59 against 82.00. Residual whitening does not explain it.

E3. BUT IT IS MOSTLY A PER-IMAGE SCALAR, NOT PER-PIXEL UNCERTAINTY. [major reframing]
    A completely FLAT log_var map, one learned value per image, reaches 79.87. Of the
    head's +13.65 over the plain AE, roughly +11.52 is a per-image scale and only
    ~+2.13 is spatial structure. Consistently, the static map explains just 39.7% of
    log_var's variance; the majority is per-image variation.
    => The mechanism is a LEARNED PER-IMAGE CALIBRATION of residual magnitude, not
       spatial uncertainty. Images that are globally harder to reconstruct get their
       residual discounted. Calling this "uncertainty" is generous and the paper must
       not lean on the spatial reading.
    => FOLLOW-UP NOW IN THE CODE (Cell 5.4): the rank correlation between that scalar
       and the image's own mean residual, plus the scalar's AUC used alone. If the
       correlation were near 1 the score would be a monotone transform of the plain L2
       score and would return 68.35 — it returns 79.87, so the scalar demonstrably
       carries independent information. The control measures how much instead of
       inferring it.

E4. THE "CAPACITY THRESHOLD" WAS NEVER A CAPACITY THRESHOLD.        [claim replaced]
    808 NONLINEAR parameters reach 78.27; 8,192 LINEAR parameters reach 66.75 — 32x the
    capacity, no better than the 256-parameter linear head (66.94). Verified from
    source: BasicBlock(..., last_layer=True) executes `layers = layers[:-2]`, stripping
    the BatchNorm and ReLU, so the original w=1 head was a single ConvTranspose2d with
    no nonlinearity at all, and the w=1 -> w=2 jump confounded width with depth,
    nonlinearity and normalisation.
    => THE CORRECT CLAIM: the variance is NOT LINEARLY DECODABLE from a frozen decoder's
       features. Joint training's function is to make it linearly readable — AE-U's own
       variance branch is a single linear ConvTranspose (256 marginal parameters) and
       reaches 86.86, which is exactly the point.

E5. POST-HOC UNCERTAINTY DOES NOT TRANSFER UNIVERSALLY.               [scope narrowed]
    It lifts the L2-trained backbone (+13.65) and the SSIM-trained one (+6.19), and
    DESTROYS the perceptually-trained one (-30.56, to 57.01). "Retrofit uncertainty onto
    any frozen reconstruction model" is dead. See §3 — the failure is predicted.

E6. AN IMAGENET-FREE ROUTE TO AE-U's ACCURACY.                        [new, practical]
    Frozen AE-SSIM + post-hoc head = 86.99 +- 0.56, against jointly-trained AE-U's
    86.86 +- 0.44, using no pre-trained weights and touching no reconstruction weight.
    It also beats AE-SSIM's own native score by +6.19 — a case where a NON-NATIVE score
    beats the diagonal (see §3).

E7. THE IRREDUCIBLE GAP IS REAL, AND THE FREE RESCORE GAIN IS REAL.  [now with statistics]
    AE-U over the post-hoc head: +4.86, p=4.3e-09. Plain AE rescored with SSIM: +9.08,
    p=7.4e-37. Post-hoc over plain AE: +13.65, p=5e-75. All by paired DeLong.

E8. THE POST-HOC HEAD DOES NOT BEAT RETRAINED AE-SSIM.                [claim retracted]
    +1.19, 95% CI [-0.15, +2.53], not significant. The earlier "beats retrained
    AE-SSIM" phrasing is withdrawn; "reaches the level of" is the supported form.


################################################################################
# 3. THE UNIFYING MECHANISM — and the correction it needs
################################################################################
The working hypothesis was: "a score reads a residual only if it lives in the space the
objective shaped it for." Tested against all the evidence, that is nearly right but
SYMMETRIC, and the data are ASYMMETRIC. The corrected statement is stronger.

THE EVIDENCE, row by row (AUC; `unc` = uncertainty score, from A4/M2):

    trained        l2     grad   perc   ssim   unc      range   below chance
    l2           68.3    73.9   75.9   77.4   82.0      13.7    none
    ssim         61.9    75.2   68.2   80.8   87.0      25.1    none
    perceptual   44.9    64.9   87.6   44.0   57.0      43.6    l2, ssim
    aeu          65.4    71.7   73.6   75.1   86.9      21.5    none

THE PATTERN
    Only ONE row contains catastrophic cells, and they are exactly the PIXEL-SPACE
    scores applied to the PERCEPTUALLY-trained model. Every other pairing is merely
    better or worse, never broken.

THE CORRECTED LAW
    An objective CONSTRAINS a subspace; a score can only read what the objective
    constrained. It is not symmetric:
      * L2 and SSIM constrain pixel intensities — and a pixel-faithful reconstruction is
        also approximately feature-faithful. So pixel-trained models are readable by
        pixel-space AND feature-space scores (AE: 68.3 / 77.4 / 75.9 / 82.0, all usable).
      * The perceptual objective constrains deep features and does NOT constrain
        absolute pixel intensity. The reconstruction is therefore free to drift in pixel
        space, and every pixel-space reading of it collapses (44.9, 44.0, 57.0).
    => pixel-constrained model  -> readable by pixel AND feature scores
       feature-constrained model -> readable by feature scores ONLY

WHAT THIS EXPLAINS, IN ONE MECHANISM
    (a) A1's grid, including why the perceptual row alone falls below chance;
    (b) why AE-U's advantage is scoring rather than reconstruction — its objective
        constrains pixels, so a better pixel-space reading is available to it;
    (c) M2's whole pattern: the uncertainty score is a PIXEL-SPACE score
        (exp(-log_var)*(x-x_hat)^2), so it lifts the two pixel-trained backbones and
        destroys the perceptual one;
    (d) quantitatively, the AE-PL post-hoc collapse (57.01) and A1's perceptual/L2 cell
        (44.9) are the SAME phenomenon — and the uncertainty weighting even partially
        rescues the pixel reading of that model, 44.9 -> 57.01, without coming close to
        its own perceptual score's 87.6.

WHERE THE SIMPLE VERSION OF THE LAW FAILS — state this, do not hide it
    "The diagonal always wins" is FALSE, twice:
      * the L2-trained model is better read by SSIM (77.4) than by L2 (68.3);
      * the SSIM-trained model is better read by the uncertainty score (86.99) than by
        SSIM (80.81).
    Within a compatible space, scores transfer freely and a non-native score can beat
    the native one. The constraint is the SPACE, not the identity of the metric.


################################################################################
# 4. STATUS OF THE PROJECT'S EARLIER CLAIMS
################################################################################
REFUTED
  R1. "A sharp capacity threshold at 0.62% of parameters."  -> nonlinearity, not capacity (E4)
  R2. "Post-hoc uncertainty is a general wrapper."          -> fails on AE-PL (E5)
  R3. "82.00 beats fully retrained AE-SSIM."                -> +1.19, n.s. (E8)
  R4. "The head learns per-pixel uncertainty."              -> mostly a per-image scalar (E3)

NARROWED
  N1. C2 applies to PIXEL-SPACE-trained backbones only.
  N2. "Uncertainty" should be described as a learned per-image residual calibration.
  N3. The recovery fraction must be quoted against both denominators: 74% from the plain
      AE's own L2 score (68.35), 49% from the free SSIM rescore of the same weights (77.4).

CONFIRMED / STRENGTHENED
  S1. A1's specialisation finding — strengthened, and now asymmetric (§3).
  S2. The 4.81-point irreducible gap — significant, p=4.3e-09 (E7).
  S3. The free SSIM rescore gain — significant, p=7.4e-37 (E7).
  S4. C2 survives its two strongest attacks (E1, E2).
  S5. Paired testing is the correct instrument: half-width 1.28 vs unpaired 1.48.

SURVIVED UNCHANGED
  The ten-method reproduction (9/10 within 1 sd); the A1 grid; the A3 ensemble result
  and its pre-registered negative control.


################################################################################
# 5. CONTRIBUTIONS AS THEY SHOULD NOW STAND
################################################################################
Versus 10_PAPER_FOUNDATION r2 the package is NARROWER IN SCOPE and SUBSTANTIALLY
BETTER SUPPORTED. The scope reduction is itself content: C2's failure on AE-PL is the
sharpest confirmation of C1 in the project, because the theory predicted the failure of
a tool we had built for another purpose.

C1  THE ASYMMETRIC SPECIALISATION LAW.                                   [primary]
    Crossing four objectives with five scores, an objective does not improve the
    reconstruction — it constrains a subspace, and a score reads only what was
    constrained. Pixel-constrained models are readable by pixel and feature scores;
    the feature-constrained model is readable by feature scores only, and falls below
    chance under pixel scores (44.9, 44.0).
    EVIDENCE: the grid; interaction terms +9.8 and +35.1 exceeding both main effects;
    the free SSIM rescore +9.08 (p=7.4e-37); and M2's independent confirmation.
    SCOPE: RSNA, image level, 64x64, image-reconstruction methods.

C2  A PROBE THAT TESTS C1 AND OBTAINS A CELL THE GRID CANNOT COMPUTE.    [primary]
    Fitting a variance head to a frozen autoencoder supplies the otherwise-undefined
    plain-AE + uncertainty-score cell, and shows AE-U's advantage is a property of its
    score, not of a better reconstruction (AE-U read with L2 = 65.4, below the plain
    AE's 68.35). It is NOT a static mask (69.10), NOT zero-parameter whitening (69.59),
    is dominated by a per-image scale rather than spatial structure (79.87 of 82.00),
    and requires NONLINEARITY rather than capacity (808 nonlinear 78.27 vs 8,192 linear
    66.75). It transfers to pixel-trained backbones and destroys the perceptual one,
    exactly as C1 predicts.
    SCOPE: a decomposition instrument, NOT a deployment recipe. It costs more wall-clock
    than training AE-U from scratch and lands 4.86 below it (p=4.3e-09).

C3  AN IMAGENET-FREE ROUTE TO THE UNCERTAINTY AUTOENCODER'S ACCURACY.  [supporting]
    Frozen AE-SSIM + post-hoc head = 86.99 +- 0.56 vs jointly-trained AE-U 86.86 +- 0.44,
    with no pre-trained weights and no reconstruction retraining, and +6.19 over
    AE-SSIM's own score. Practically relevant because AE-PL, the strongest single
    method, depends entirely on ImageNet features.

C4  SCORE COMPLEMENTARITY.                                             [supporting]
    perceptual + uncertainty = 88.74 +- 0.13, held-out 88.83, selected in 100% of 150
    split-half selections, reported with its pre-registered negative control (both
    pre-registered ensembles lost: 84.88, 81.91). Rank averaging is textbook
    (Aggarwal & Sathe 2015; Zimek et al. 2014) — present as evidence for C1 and as a
    demonstration of selection-aware validation, never as a method.
    NOTE: the DeLong test for this comparison has not yet been run (see §1 bug note).

The reproduction remains Experimental Setup, not a numbered contribution.
