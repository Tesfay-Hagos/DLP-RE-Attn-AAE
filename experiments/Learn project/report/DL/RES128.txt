==============================================================================
THE 128px REPLICATION — a prediction made in print, then tested
==============================================================================

SUPERSEDES the first version of this file, which compared the VALID-CONVOLUTION
128px deltas against FULL-SUPPORT predictions and reported errors of 0.31/0.72/6.48.
Those are not like for like. The appendix bandwidth rows -- and therefore the
sigma=0.75 prediction -- are replicate-padded at full support; the library default
is valid convolution and carries a support term as well. The full-support arm at
128px has since been measured on the same frozen weights, and the corrected errors
are 3.49 / 1.15 / 3.07 -- more uniform, and LAG is an ordinary 3-point miss rather
than the 35% blowout the earlier column implied.

SETUP. Plain l2-objective autoencoder retrained at 128x128, 250 epochs, 3 seeds,
namespace res128-v1-*, disjoint from the 64px dl-v1 runs. Rescored two ways on the
frozen weights: library-default SSIM (valid conv) and replicate-padded full support.

WHAT IS SUPPORTED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * The distance component moved in the predicted direction on all three, by
    -10.95, -7.43 and -14.96, against predicted -14.44, -8.58 and -11.89.
  * Errors of 1.15 to 3.49 on movements of 7 to 15 points, from a first-principles
    scale argument, at a resolution never trained on, with parameter count tripling
    in between.
  * The reported (valid-conv) RSNA result flips: +9.08 at 64px, -3.00 at 128px, all
    three seeds negative. The printed prediction that the gain vanishes holds.
  * At 128px all three reported deltas are negative.

WHAT IS NOT SUPPORTED — this is the finding, not the embarrassment
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * RSNA's distance component lands at +0.18 (sd 0.30), indistinguishable from
    zero, where the bandwidth account predicted -3.31. So the observed RSNA sign
    flip is carried by the SUPPORT term (-3.18), not by the distance change the
    bandwidth argument concerns. The account predicted the disappearance of the
    +11.13 gain, not a harmful distance, and only the weaker statement is earned.
  * The paper's own distance/support decomposition is what caught this, applied to
    the authors' own mechanism and against their own interest.
  * The prediction is one pattern ('all negative'), not three independent events.
    Any mechanism degrading SSIM at higher resolution yields the same 3/3.
  * LAG's valid-conv SSIM lands at 52.07, near chance. A monotone unbounded
    bandwidth account cannot predict a floor and is not tested by an observation
    pinned at one.

CONFOUND THAT MUST BE DISCLOSED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  n_params 2,351,184 -> 8,645,712 (3.68x): the bottleneck FC scales with input size.
  Resolution and capacity moved together. Within each resolution BOTH arms share
  identical weights, so capacity cannot produce the l2-versus-SSIM delta except
  through an interaction with the score. The l2 baselines move by at most 2.0 AUROC
  across the resolution change while the SSIM arm moves by up to 24.9. A
  latent-matched 128px run would separate them; we have not run it. Note the paper
  indicts Bergmann et al. for moving two things and crediting one -- say this
  plainly rather than let a reviewer draw the parallel.

A CONVENTION NOTE ON THE 64px SUPPORT TERM
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  sigma_crossing.py clamps to [0,1]; ssim_window_sweep.py does not, and the 64px
  ssim_valid is taken from the latter. Empirically this is a no-op: the main
  pipeline's own clamped run a1-l2-ssim gives 77.4215 on RSNA, matching the
  unclamped window sweep to four decimals. Two paths, same number.
