# 2. ABSTRACT SKELETON
################################################################################
Reconstruction-based anomaly detectors are reported as single methods, but each makes
two separable decisions: what the model is trained to reconstruct, and how the residual
is turned into a score. Crossing four objectives with five scores on one backbone and
one protocol (RSNA, 64x64, 3 seeds), we find the two are not additive: the interaction
exceeds both main effects (+9.8 for AE-SSIM, +35.1 for AE-PL). A training objective does
not produce a better reconstruction — it SPECIALISES the residual so that its own metric
can read it. Scoring a metric-trained model with plain pixel error falls below a plain
autoencoder in every case we measure (61.9, 65.4, and 44.9 and 44.0, both below chance).
Scores, by contrast, transfer cheaply: a plain autoencoder rescored with SSIM reaches
77.4 against its own 68.35, without retraining. We then obtain a cell the grid cannot
otherwise compute — a plain autoencoder read with an uncertainty score — by fitting a
variance head to a FROZEN autoencoder. It reaches 82.00, recovering 74% of the
uncertainty autoencoder's advantage measured from the plain baseline, or 49% measured
from the same weights rescored with SSIM; a purely linear head fails at 66.94. The
remaining 4.81 points do not close across an 11.6x capacity range, and we attribute them
to reconstruction-variance co-adaptation during joint training.

    Final sentence of the abstract must be the reporting-norm recommendation (§8 R2).
    The 88.74 ensemble does NOT appear in the abstract — see §5 S5 and §7 C4.

C1 — FACTORIAL DECOMPOSITION OF OBJECTIVE x SCORE          [primary]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STATEMENT
    We cross four training objectives with five anomaly scores on one backbone and one
    protocol, and attribute each published method's gain to the objective, the score,
    and their interaction. The two factors are NOT separable: the interaction is the
    largest component. An objective does not improve the reconstruction; it specialises
    the residual so its own metric can read it.

EVIDENCE
    * interaction exceeds both main effects: +9.8 (AE-SSIM), +35.1 (AE-PL)
    * every train-only term is NEGATIVE: -6.4 (SSIM), -2.9 (uncertainty),
      -23.4 (perceptual) — metric-trained models are WORSE reconstructors in pixel terms
    * scoring a metric-trained model with L2 falls below the plain AE in every case:
      61.9, 65.4, and 44.9; 44.0 is a second below-chance cell (perceptual/SSIM)
    * scores transfer: plain AE rescored with SSIM 68.35 -> 77.4; rescored with the
      gradient score -> 73.86. For a plain AE, L2 is the WORST of its readable scores.
    * axis spreads: score 26.7 vs objective 14.2 (report as secondary; the interaction
      is the headline, not this ratio)

NOVELTY — see §3. Verified against [P1]-[P5] plus an industrial/natural-image AD search
    ([P6], [P7], Bionda et al. DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-06430-2_56, Meissen et al. FAE
     DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-33842-7_2). No prior work crosses the reconstruction metric.

DO NOT CLAIM (r1 error, now corrected)
    r1 asserted "breadth and peak are inversely related", citing AE-U as capping at
    75.1. FALSE: 75.1 is AE-U's best NON-uncertainty score; its own diagonal is 86.9,
    the second-highest cell in the grid. AE-U has a NARROW 4-column range AND a very
    high peak, which is a direct counterexample. The inverse relation holds for the
    perceptual row only and must not be stated as a general property.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C2 — WHAT THE UNCERTAINTY SCORE ACTUALLY MEASURES              [primary, NEW in r3]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STATEMENT
    AE-U's score is not a measure of reconstruction error, and not a measure of
    uncertainty in any loose sense. It is RESIDUAL RELATIVE TO EXPECTED DIFFICULTY. The
    head learns how hard an image SHOULD be to reconstruct; the anomaly signal is the gap
    between the actual residual and that expectation.

EVIDENCE — the two directions disagree, and that is the finding
    POOLED  corr(log_var, residual) = +0.506
            Across the population, harder images get a LARGER predicted log_var and are
            DISCOUNTED. The head has learned expected difficulty.
    BETWEEN log_var(abnormal) - log_var(normal) = -0.0997
            Abnormal images get a SMALLER log_var, i.e. weight 182.5 vs 165.2 (+10.5%),
            so they are discounted LESS than their residual alone would predict.
      => score = mean(exp(-log_var) * r^2) == residual relative to expected difficulty
           normal-but-hard : high r^2, HIGH log_var -> ratio moderate
           abnormal        : high r^2, LOW  log_var -> ratio large
    It also explains the otherwise-awkward standalone number: -log_var alone reaches only
    64.42 because the class alignment in the weight is small (+10.5%). The discriminative
    quantity is the RATIO, not either factor.

SUPPORTING — the alternatives are excluded, not assumed
    NOT a static anatomical mask   : the train-mean map alone gives 69.10 vs 82.00
    NOT zero-parameter whitening   : r^2 / mean-train-residual gives 69.59
    NOT redundant with the residual: |rho| = 0.506, far from 1
    NOT linearly decodable         : 808 NONLINEAR params reach 78.27 while 8,192 LINEAR
                                     params reach 66.75 — the earlier "capacity
                                     threshold" was confounded and is retracted

WHY IT MATTERS
    Every uncertainty-based detector in this family is described as "weighting the
    residual by predicted uncertainty". This says what that weighting is doing, and it
    predicts where it will fail — which M2 then confirms (C3).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C3 — THE MECHANISM PREDICTS ITS OWN TOOL'S FAILURE                    [primary]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STATEMENT
    Fitting the variance head to a FROZEN backbone obtains the grid cell A1 cannot
    compute, and its behaviour across backbones follows C1's law exactly: the uncertainty
    score is a PIXEL-SPACE score, so it lifts pixel-trained backbones and destroys the
    feature-trained one.

EVIDENCE
    frozen ae       68.35 -> 82.00 +- 0.83   +13.65   (p=5e-75)
    frozen ae-ssim  80.81 -> 86.99 +- 0.57    +6.19   -> matches AE-U's 86.86, no ImageNet
    frozen ae-pl    87.58 -> 57.01 +- 2.14   -30.56   -> the predicted collapse
    AE-U jointly trained 86.86; the post-hoc route stays 4.86 short (p=4.3e-09)

    A theory that predicts the failure of a tool built for another purpose is stronger
    than one that only explains successes. This is the same phenomenon as A1's
    perceptual/L2 cell (44.9).

SUBSTITUTABILITY (Item 4, pre-registered as |difference| < 1.5)
    perceptual + jointly-trained AE-U       88.74
    perceptual + frozen AE-SSIM + head      89.12    difference +0.38, p=0.788
    => no detectable difference; the head substitutes for AE-U AS A COMPONENT.
    SCOPE: this removes the need for uncertainty TRAINING, not for ImageNet — the
    perceptual member still requires VGG-19. And 89.12 may not be reported as beating
    88.74. This is failure-to-reject, not demonstrated equivalence.

C4 — COMPLEMENTARY SCORES BEAT THE BEST IMAGE-RECONSTRUCTION METHOD   [supporting]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8B. THE ABANDONED MULTI-LOSS EXTENSION — why, and what to keep
################################################################################
Proposal: weighted sum of uncertainty-weighted L2 + SSIM + perceptual, one shared
uncertainty map across pixel and perceptual terms, mixture score divided by variance,
optional memory + cross-attention bottleneck. REJECTED for four independent reasons:

(a) PRIOR ART. Learned variance weighting multiple heterogeneous losses is
    [P16] Kendall, A., Gal, Y., Cipolla, R. CVPR 2018. DOI: 10.1109/cvpr.2018.00781
    Weighted L1/L2/SSIM mixtures are
    [P17] Zhao, H., Gallo, O., Frosio, I., Kautz, J. IEEE TCI 2017.
          DOI: 10.1109/tci.2016.2644865
    Memory + cross-attention for CXR AD is
    [P18] Tian, Y., et al. "MemMC-MAE." arXiv:2203.11725 (MLMI @ MICCAI 2023)
          (NOTE: arXiv:2203.11495 is an unrelated physics paper — do not cite it)
    [P19] Gong, D., et al. MemAE. ICCV 2019. DOI: 10.1109/ICCV.2019.00179
    [P20] Park, H., Noh, J., Ham, B. CVPR 2020. DOI: 10.1109/cvpr42600.2020.01438
(b) OUR OWN EVIDENCE CONTRADICTS TWO COMPONENTS. Adding SSIM to a perceptual score
    LOWERED AUC both times measured (86.69 vs 87.58; 88.09 vs 88.74). Every
    bottleneck-side lever moved nothing (AE 68.35, VAE 67.33, AE-l1 68.53 — inside seed
    noise), while everything that beat that cluster changed the SCORE.
(c) THE SHARED UNCERTAINTY MAP IS NOT WELL DEFINED. sigma^2 is 64x64x1; VGG relu4_2 is
    8x8x512 with a near-global receptive field. Weighting feature residuals by a pooled
    pixel variance destroys the Gaussian likelihood that gives +log sigma^2 its meaning,
    and the pooling rule (mean of log-variance vs variance vs precision) is an
    undeclared hyperparameter with no principled answer.
(d) UNTUNABLE. 4-5 continuous hyperparameters with no validation set (S6). Selecting on

    gain. C3's split-half protocol does NOT transfer: it was credible because the space
    was 15 enumerated options with a unanimous winner, not a continuous 5-D grid.

point. And there is a real argument the other way: a small pixel term added to a

hypothesised behind the 44.9 cell. One row, ~5 min, reported as an ablation.
