================================================================================
EXPERIMENT A3 (NOVEL)
Do different anomaly scores read different things? Combining them, free.
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. MOTIVATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A1 showed the scoring function is the dominant lever. That raises an immediate question:
do the different scores read the SAME signal through different lenses, or do they read
DIFFERENT parts of the residual?

    * SSIM reads local structure — variance and covariance in an 11x11 window
    * the perceptual distance reads semantic/textural mismatch in VGG feature space
    * AE-U's score reads residual relative to the model's own predicted uncertainty

If these are complementary, combining them should beat any one of them. If they are
redundant, combining changes nothing. Either answer is informative.

COST: zero training. Every run in Step 1 already stored its per-image score vector, so
this experiment only loads and combines arrays.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. METHOD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RANK AVERAGING, not score averaging.

    The four scores live on incompatible scales — L2 around 1e-2, perceptual around 0.3,
    AE-U's is negative. A plain mean would simply return whichever member has the largest
    numerical range. Each score vector is therefore converted to ranks in [0,1] and the
    ranks are averaged. AUC and AP depend only on ordering, so nothing is lost by
    discarding the magnitudes.

PRE-REGISTRATION — the methodological point of this experiment.

    With 4 members there are 15 non-empty subsets. Trying all 15 on the test set and
    reporting the best one is test-set fitting; it would not survive review and it would
    not generalise. So two combinations were declared IN CODE before any result was seen:

      all4 = L2 + SSIM + perceptual + uncertainty
             the assumption-free choice: use everything.
      noIN = L2 + SSIM + uncertainty
             pre-registered because it answers an independent question. AE-PL, the best
             single method, needs ImageNet weights. If a combination without any
             pre-trained component reaches its accuracy, that is practically useful
             wherever ImageNet pre-training is unavailable or unwanted.

    All 15 subsets are still computed and printed, but explicitly labelled EXPLORATORY.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. RESULT PART 1 — THE PRE-REGISTERED COMBINATIONS BOTH FAILED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    all4   L2+SSIM+perceptual+uncertainty   84.88 +- 0.25    vs best single 87.58  (-2.69)
    noIN   L2+SSIM+uncertainty              81.91 +- 0.36    vs best single 87.58  (-5.66)

    This is a genuine negative result and it should be reported as prominently as the
    positive one. Pre-registering is what makes it credible rather than embarrassing.

    THE REASON IS VISIBLE AND GENERAL: the L2 member poisons the average.
        subsets CONTAINING L2 : mean 80.08
        subsets WITHOUT L2    : mean 86.44
    The L2 score is 19 points weaker than the others, and equal-weight rank averaging
    has no mechanism to discount a weak member — every vote counts the same.

    LESSON: naive ensembling of anomaly scores is not safe. Combination requires either
    a weighting scheme or a principled member-selection rule, which is exactly what the
    next part supplies.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. RESULT PART 2 — THE FULL SCAN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    combination                        k    AUC
    ------------------------------------------------
    perceptual + uncertainty           2   88.74 +- 0.13    <- best overall
    SSIM + perceptual + uncertainty    3   88.09 +- 0.22
    perceptual                         1   87.58 +- 0.32    (AE-PL alone)
    uncertainty                        1   86.86 +- 0.53    (AE-U alone)
    SSIM + perceptual                  2   86.69 +- 0.12
    SSIM + uncertainty                 2   86.33 +- 0.43
    L2 + perceptual + uncertainty      3   85.00 +- 0.36
    L2 + SSIM + perceptual + uncert    4   84.88 +- 0.25    (pre-registered "all4")
    L2 + SSIM + perceptual             3   82.31 +- 0.21
    L2 + SSIM + uncertainty            3   81.91 +- 0.36    (pre-registered "noIN")
    L2 + perceptual                    2   81.46 +- 0.50
    SSIM                               1   80.81 +- 0.43
    L2 + uncertainty                   2   80.78 +- 0.57
    L2 + SSIM                          2   75.98 +- 0.15
    L2                                 1   68.35 +- 0.80

    The top combination, perceptual + uncertainty, beats every single method. Its seed
    spread (0.13) is the tightest in the table, and the next two best combinations also
    contain that pair — so it is not one lucky cell.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. RESULT PART 3 — SELECTION-AWARE VALIDATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
88.74 cannot be reported as it stands: it is the best of 15 options chosen on the test
set. To measure what that selection is actually worth, the choice was repeated under a
protocol that never lets the winner see the data it is scored on.

PROTOCOL
    Repeat 50 times per seed (150 runs total):
      1. split the 2000 test images into two stratified halves A and B (both keep the
         50/50 class balance, so an AUC on either half is comparable to the full set),
      2. evaluate all 15 combinations on half A and select the best,
      3. report THAT combination's AUC on half B, which it has never seen.
    The gap between step 2's number and step 3's number IS the selection bias, measured
    rather than assumed away.

RESULT
    oracle   (best combo, scored on the half it was chosen on)   88.64   <- optimistic
    held-out (that same combo on the UNSEEN half)                88.83   <- honest
    AE-PL alone on the same half (no selection at all)           87.66   <- baseline

    selection bias                     -0.20 AUC points
    honest gain over AE-PL             +1.18 AUC points
    held-out beats AE-PL in            100% of 150 splits
    most frequently selected combo     perceptual + uncertainty, in 100% of splits

INTERPRETATION
    The selection bias is effectively ZERO, and the reason is the important part: the
    SAME combination wins in every single split. There is nothing to overfit when the
    choice is unambiguous. Held-out even edges slightly above oracle, which is ordinary
    noise around a stable winner.

    The finding therefore survives. perceptual+uncertainty is a real +1.18 over the best
    single method, and +1.33 over the best published image-reconstruction result (87.5).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHAT TO CLAIM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CLAIM (supported):
    "A rank-average of the perceptual and uncertainty scores reaches 88.74 AUC, beating
     the best single reconstruction method by 1.16 points at zero additional training
     cost, and it wins on held-out data in 100% of 150 selection splits."

CLAIM (supported, and report it alongside):
    "Naive equal-weight ensembling FAILS: both pre-registered combinations lost to the
     best single method, because a weak member drags the average down."

DO NOT CLAIM:
    * that ensembling helps in general — it demonstrably does not (see the L2 subsets)
    * that this generalises beyond RSNA. The validation is within-dataset. The decisive
      test is whether the same pair wins on a different dataset, which is the single
      most valuable remaining experiment.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHY THE WINNING PAIR MAKES SENSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Not proven here, but worth stating as the natural reading: the two winning members
    are the two that read the MOST DIFFERENT things. The perceptual score reads semantic
    mismatch in a feature space learned from natural images; the uncertainty score reads
    pixel residual normalised by the model's own expected difficulty. They also come from
    opposite ends of the pre-training question — one depends on ImageNet entirely, the
    other not at all. Their combination beating both is consistent with them making
    different errors.
